Xiaogang village · Shenzhen · 1978–2024

Run your own SEZ

In 1978 a Chinese farmer asked: “Do I own the teeth in my head?” The answer was no — even his teeth belonged to the collective.

Deng Xiaoping’s answer wasn’t to change the whole country. It was to fence off one small place, change only its rules, and let the result do the arguing. This page lets you run that experiment yourself — first in a village, then in a city — with the real numbers history recorded.

Winter 1978 · Xiaogang village
The secret contract

18 farmers signed by oil lamp in a mud hut, knowing it could get them executed. One called it “like high voltage wire — touch it and you die.” The contract was hidden inside bamboo in a roof. The village had already lost 67 of its 120 people to hunger in 1958–60.

The clause they wrote in: if any of them was imprisoned or executed for this, the rest would raise his children until they turned 18.
26 Aug 1980 · The special economic zones
The fenced city

Four zones were approved on one day: Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Shantou and Xiamen. Shenzhen sat directly across from Hong Kong, and admission was initially fenced off and selective — a small place where different rules could be tested before anyone bet the country on them.

The push came from Guangdong officials asking for “special policies and flexible measures.” Deng approved.

“Work hard, don’t work hard — everyone gets the same. So people don’t want to work.”

— Yen Jingchang, Xiaogang farmer, on life before the contract (NPR, 2012). Before, farmers only went out when the village whistle blew. After, families were in the fields before dawn, secretly competing.

The method: feeling the stones

Deng named his own approach late: “crossing the river by feeling the stones.” Test small, copy what works. Historian Ezra Vogel distilled the playbook into five moves:

Experiment in SEZs before scaling anything nationwide.
Cross the river by feeling the stones — small steps, checked as you go.
Send students abroad to learn.
Learn from Singapore and Japan.
Let numbers, not ideology, settle arguments.

What actually happened

1978
Winter. 18 Xiaogang farmers sign the secret contract by oil lamp and hide it in bamboo in a roof.
1979
The harvest gives them away: 90,000 kg of grain, over 6× the year before. Income per person goes ¥22 → ¥400. Yen Hongchang is hauled into the Party office like a man on death row — Deng holds the farmers up as a model instead.
1980
26 August: Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Shantou and Xiamen approved as Special Economic Zones. Shenzhen starts at ¥270 million GDP and about 330,000 people.
1992
Deng, aged 87, tours Shenzhen on his Southern Tour and reignites reform.
2024
Shenzhen’s GDP reaches ¥3.68 trillion — roughly 13,600× its 1980 level — and it is now China’s third-largest city economy, at about $560 billion slightly bigger than Vietnam’s.
500 million

people have risen out of poverty in China since 1978. (NPR)

  • Shenzhen GDP: ¥270 million (1980) → ¥3.68 trillion (2024). One source prints ¥2.7 billion for 1980; this page uses ¥270 million, the figure the film uses.
  • Shenzhen population: about 330,000 in 1980 → 17–18 million today; this page shows 17 million, matching the film.
  • Stalled tiles are deliberate: they stop at the recorded starting numbers. History never ran those rule mixes, so this page doesn’t invent end numbers for them.
